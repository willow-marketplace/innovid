# How this is ordered and labelled — no score

**There is no score in this skill.** No index, no points, no 0–100, no weighted components. Earlier versions defined one; it is gone deliberately.

**What is banned, and what isn't.** The problem is *weighted multi-variable indexes* — a number built by multiplying or summing five or nine components with invented weights, then surfaced to the advisor. Those are gone everywhere, permanently. Any figure of the form "this is worth twice that" is a guess presented as measurement, the first thing anyone shown it asks is how it was calculated, and the honest answer — "we picked those weights" — destroys the credibility of everything else on the page.

**Plain counts, dates, labels and priority order are welcome.** "Four of the six items need someone outside the firm" · "raised in four of their last five meetings" · "168 days, about three times their usual rhythm" · "cooling" · "act now" · "confirm this first" — all fine, all useful, all instantly checkable by the advisor. The test is simple: **can the advisor verify it by looking, and re-decide it themselves if they disagree?** A count of things they can see passes. An index derived from weights they can't see does not.

What replaces it: **named signals, an explicit priority order, and plain-language labels assigned by condition.** Deterministic, so two runs on the same data agree, and explainable in the sentence that is also the rule.

---

## Why a score was especially wrong here

This skill's output is a single recommendation an advisor may act on with a client's money. An Action Confidence Score of 62 implies a precision the underlying data does not have — the inputs are extracted conversation facts of varying age, not measurements — and it papers over the one thing that actually decides the answer: **whether the window closes**. An irreversible pension election two months out and a tax-drag inefficiency are not comparable quantities, and forcing them onto one axis is how the tax-drag item wins on volume.

The playbook's triage order already encodes the right judgement. Use it directly.

## The signals, per surviving candidate

| Signal | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Reversibility** | Can this decision be undone later? A pension election, a claiming decision, a beneficiary designation at death — no. Most portfolio decisions — yes |
| **Whether the window closes, and when** | A dated deadline · a calendar-year or event-keyed window · open-ended |
| **What it acts on** | The stated balance, income, or exposure the mechanism touches, quoted as the client stated it with its date. Where the record cannot support a figure, say exactly what data would produce one — that gap is often the deliverable |
| **Verified or assumed** | Whether every fact the rule needs is actually on record. Any gap turns the action into **verify / check / ask** — recommending the investigation is correct; asserting the conclusion is not |
| **Whether the door is open** | Did the household raise adjacent territory themselves? Untouched territory is a harder conversation, not a worse idea |
| **Whether they're in a state to hear it** | An unresolved concern or unacknowledged life event at the last meeting means they are not ready for an unrelated recommendation, however good it is. Leading with one spends trust the advisor needs |

## The order — the playbook's triage chain, applied in sequence

1. **Irreversible, with a deadline.** Once it passes, no later advice recovers it.
2. **A window that closes on a date** — a conversion year, an enrolment period, a tax year end.
3. **Unpriced catastrophic risk** — a protection or estate gap where the downside is ruin rather than inefficiency.
4. **Compounding inefficiency** — real, quantifiable, and still there next quarter.
5. **Relationship and documentation** — beneficiary sweeps, risk-tolerance refreshes, compliance touches.

Within a tier, order by what it acts on, largest stated exposure first. **Merge before ordering**: rules sharing one deliverable are one recommendation — catch-up plus 457(b) is one deferral analysis, and conversion window plus IRMAA plus the survivor's filing status is one multi-year tax plan. That merge is often the strongest recommendation available.

**Two overrides:**

- **A hard dated deadline always surfaces**, even when everything about it is otherwise minor.
- **Where the last meeting carried an unresolved concern or an unacknowledged life event**, an unrelated recommendation is demoted below one that engages what is already on their mind — and if nothing engages it, say that plainly instead of leading with something else.

## The words

- **Do this now** — irreversible or dated inside roughly six months.
- **This month** — a window that closes this calendar year or on a known event.
- **Raise at the next review** — real, quantified, not time-critical.
- **Nothing material outstanding** — the honest answer when the record supports it, with the reason. This is a valid and sometimes correct output; padding it with generic advice is not.

## Firm context

The firmwide insights read is **context, printed once and labelled "across the firm"** — whether comparable territory is live across the firm right now. It never enters the order. Where insights are unavailable, say the firm read was unavailable and drop the line; never invent it, and never rebuild it from the meetings tools.
